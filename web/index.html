    How it works

    The brain

    We took the complete male fruit fly central nervous system (Janelia & Google, 2026) and kept every traced neuron on a short path from the eyes to the descending neurons, plus the mushroom body, the fly's learning center. That's 79,992 neurons and 4.27 million connections. Each one is a spiking leaky integrate-and-fire neuron (Shiu et al. 2024 parameters). Its sign (excitatory or inhibitory) comes from the predicted neurotransmitter.

    The eyes

    The game is rendered in 3D from the fly's head onto 4,363 lamina neurons (L1, L2, L3), each at the viewing direction of its column. Pipes are pillars with a slot. From there, everything the optic lobe does is the connectome's own wiring. A developmental phase (homeostatic synaptic scaling while watching flight scenes) set each neuron's input gain once; the gains are frozen during play.

    Learning

    Every 40 ms the fly chooses to flap or glide. When things go better or worse than expected, the surprise (a TD error) is injected as reward into PAM or punishment into PPL1 dopamine neurons. Their spikes change the Kenyon cell → MBON synapses, just as in real flies. After each crash the fly replays recent moments of flight (like rest-time consolidation), which is what makes a few hundred lives enough.

    What's biology, what's not

    The connectome has no nerve cord circuits for wing control, and its real Kenyon cells barely see where the gap is. So in Hybrid mode, a learned readout stands in for those premotor circuits. It reads the brain's visual populations (compressed to their main components during development) and a copy of the last wing beat. In Biology only mode, the DNg02 wing-power neurons decide alone, and the fly never learns to pass a pipe. The mushroom body does learn from the same dopamine, but its output neurons don't know where the gap is, so those memories can't steer the wings. The innate attraction to the opening is a small extra reward for getting closer to the gap: the teacher knows where the gap is, the fly doesn't.
